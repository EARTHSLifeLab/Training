Section 5 of 10

Types of Water

Three types of water are used in the Life Lab. Using the right water for the right purpose is critical to the health of the animals.

Reverse Osmosis (RO) Water

Plain white jugs
Location: Under the counter near the Reef Tank

Used for:

  • The Chahoua gecko's automated misting system
  • The reef tank reservoir
  • The handheld pump mister

Reptile Water

The jug with red stripes
Location: On the counter near the Dubias

Used for:

  • All animal water dishes
  • Moistening substrate

To make more reptile water:

  1. Fill the jug with tap water
  2. Add conditioner per the bottle directions
  3. Close the lid and turn once to mix
  4. Ready to use

Chlorine and chloramines in tap water are harmful to reptiles. Frogs are especially vulnerable - they breathe through their skin!

Tap Water

Used for washing dishes only.

Important Tap water should never be added directly to an animal's enclosure.
Section 6 of 10

Dish Washing

All dishes are washed in the prep room using hot water and set out to air-dry.

Reef Tank Warning

There is a specific food cup for the reef tank. It must NEVER be washed with soap.

Please hand rinse only.

Soap residue can cause Total Tank Collapse - a fatal condition for all fish and coral.

Soap

Dawn dish soap is the safest option for reptile-safe cleaning.

Dawn is highly concentrated ~ Always dilute it before use.

It's not an exact ratio - add just enough soap that the water turns light blue. That's it!

Sponges

Sponge Warning! Never use AMUSE sponges on animal dishes. They contain harmful paint and clay residue. The Life Lab sponges are the "Scrub Mommy" sponges.

Food Prep

Frozen foods, fresh produce, and prepared animal foods are kept in the refrigerator and freezer in the prep room.

Fresh Produce

A rotating mix of fruits and vegetables is always available. Chop and prep as needed.

Two knives are provided:

  • A standard knife for adults
  • A serrated plastic kid-safe knife for pint-sized helpers

Prep supplies are in the drawer next to the fridge.

Blue Tongue Skink Food

Pellets are kept in the fridge in small portions. Additional pellets are stored in the freezer to prevent spoilage.

Gecko Puree

Stored as a powder - mix one part powder with two parts water.

Freshness Check! The puree spoils quickly - it should smell like mangoes. If it smells sour, check the log to see how old it is. If it is past 5 days, make a fresh batch, dispose of the old one, and note it in the daily log. Fresh puree is typically good for ~7 days.
Pro Tip: Prep all animal foods at the same time before entering the lab. It saves time, reduces the risk of getting locked out, and keeps everything organized.
Section 8 of 10

Live Feeders

Several animals in the Life Lab eat live bugs. Handling live feeders is one of the most important skills to get right.

The Most Important Rule
Bugs must be no larger than the space between the animal's eyes. Feeding oversized bugs can cause choking or even break the animal's jaw. When in doubt, go smaller.

Dubias

The Life Lab maintains a live dubia colony as our primary feeder insect. Select the right size for each animal you are feeding.

✓
Too small is okay
✗
Too big is not
Pro Tip: Try tapping a small number of dubias into a separate cup first - it's much easier to select the right size without the whole colony on the move.

A note about Crickets:

Crickets are not stored in the lab. Lab specialists will provide all cricket feedings referenced in the care and feeding instructions.

Section 9 of 10

Putting It All Together

Every visit follows the same basic flow. The binder will walk you through the specifics.

When You Arrive
  1. Retrieve the keys from the lockbox on the flagpole
  2. Let yourself in
  3. Put the lanyard around your neck
  4. Review the binder for updates
During Your Visit
  1. Work through each enclosure following the binder instructions
  2. Record observations on the daily log as you go
  3. Return all food and supplies to their proper place
Pro Tip: Grouping like steps can help save time. For example:
  • Prepping all frozen food cups at once
  • Clearing all dirty dishes in one pass
  • Misting all enclosures that require it together
Before You Leave
  1. Place completed log sheets in the binder
  2. Wash your hands
  3. Return the keys to the lockbox
Hand Sanitizer Should Not Be Used
District policy requires it to be present - however it does not kill salmonella and the chemicals are harmful to the animals. Always wash with soap and water.
